# The synthetic data model

Everything in this repository runs on data generated by `src/gil/simulate.py` from the parameters in `src/gil/config.py`. Nothing is estimated from real players, and no real hand histories were used. This page lists what the generator assumes, so that a reader can judge which results come from the models and which come from these assumptions.

## What is simulated

This is a decision-level simulation, not a poker engine. There are no cards, pots or hands. A player's record is a list of sessions and, inside each session, a sample of decision points (about 10 to 30 per hour of play).

Each decision point has:

- a **difficulty** in [0, 1], drawn uniformly. It stands for how hard the spot is for a human (a close river bluff-catch is hard, folding trash preflop is easy);
- three actions (fold, call, raise) with **EVs**. One action is best; the others are worse by an exponential gap (mean 1 big blind);
- a **reference distribution** over the actions: a softmax of the EVs at a low temperature (0.15). It plays the role of a solver's output: almost always the best action, sometimes a close second.

A player either follows the reference or makes a **free choice**, a softmax at a high temperature (1.0), so mistakes still lean towards less costly actions. The probability of a free choice is

    base_error + difficulty_error * difficulty + fatigue_per_hour * hours_into_session

clipped to [0, 0.95]. The recorded **EV loss** is the EV of the best action minus the EV of the action taken.

The **decision time** is lognormal with median

    time_median_s * exp(time_difficulty * difficulty + time_fatigue * hours_into_session)

and spread `time_sigma`. Players within a population differ: each player's error rates, time median and time-difficulty slope are scaled by a random factor (log-normal, sd 0.35; 0.2 for bots).

**Sessions**: each player plays a random number of sessions, each of a random length, starting at an hour drawn around a personal preferred hour (or uniformly over the day for bots).

**Tables**: players are grouped into stake pools of about 60. In each session a player meets five opponents drawn from the same pool, and each decision is made against one of them (the "villain" column). A player who is part of a pair finds the partner at the table with a set probability.

## The populations

Default counts (the checks and tests use a quarter of these):

| Segment | Players | Role | Signature in the generator |
|---|---|---|---|
| recreational | 1,400 | legitimate | Many mistakes (base error 0.35), slowish and noisy timing, short sessions (0.5 to 3 h) |
| regular | 900 | legitimate | Few mistakes on easy spots (0.04) but many more on hard ones; time grows strongly with difficulty (slope 1.3); fatigue raises both errors and time; sessions of 1.5 to 7 h |
| multi_tabler | 300 | legitimate | Fast (median 1.4 s), weaker time-difficulty link, 30 decisions per hour, long sessions |
| bot | 160 | positive (player task) | Almost no mistakes (0.01) and no link between difficulty and error; timing that does not depend on difficulty, with very low spread (sigma 0.08); sessions of 8 to 20 h starting at any hour |
| rta | 160 | positive (player task) | A regular (with base error 0.08 instead of 0.04) who, on spots with difficulty at least 0.6, copies the reference half the time and then takes an extra 3 s (sd 1.5 s) |
| colluder | 100 (50 pairs) | positive (pair task) | Regulars in fixed pairs who sit together in 70% of sessions; a raise against the partner becomes a call half the time (soft play); 30% of pairs also chip dump (one partner picks the worst action against the other 30% of the time) |

Legitimate pairs exist too: 80 pairs of regulars are "friends" who sit together in 60% of sessions but play normally against each other. They are there so that sitting together is not, on its own, enough to separate colluders.

## Observable columns and ground truth

`src/gil/data.py` marks every column as observable (what an operator's logs could hold: account id, stake pool, session start and length, per-decision difficulty, best action, action taken, EV loss, time, hours into the session, villain id) or ground truth (segment, pair id and kind, chip dumper, and the per-decision policy: own play, mistake, assisted, soft play or dump).

Features may read observable columns only. The check `features_ignore_ground_truth` scrambles every ground-truth column and requires every feature to stay the same.

Two simplifications are worth stating. Difficulty and the best action are given, as if a solver had labelled every decision; in practice both would be estimates with their own error. And EV loss is measured against the same reference the RTA users copy, which makes RTA easier to see than it would be if the tool and the reference disagreed.

## Features

Per player (`player_features`), in three groups:

- **accuracy**: mean EV loss overall, on easy spots (difficulty below 1/3) and on hard spots (at least 2/3); `loss_gap` (hard minus easy); share of decisions matching the best action; `fatigue_gap` (EV loss after two hours into a session minus in the first hour).
- **timing**: mean log decision time; coefficient of variation of time; correlation between log time and difficulty; `time_hard_minus_easy` (mean log time on hard spots minus easy).
- **schedule**: mean and maximum session length; `hour_spread` (1 minus the length of the mean vector of session start hours on the 24-hour circle: 0 when every session starts at the same hour, near 1 when starts are spread around the clock); decisions per hour.

Per pair (`pair_features`), for pairs who met in at least 5 sessions: shared sessions, `together_share` (shared sessions over both players' sessions), decisions between them, `min_raise_ratio` and `mean_raise_ratio` (each player's raise rate against the other relative to their raise rate against everyone else, smoothed), and `loss_vs_partner_gap` (the larger of the two players' extra EV loss against the partner).

## Splits

Players are split into train, validation and test (60/20/20) by stake pool. Opponents come from the same pool, so every pair of players who met ends up in one split, and no player is in two. A pair is evaluated in a split when both players belong to it.

## Robustness scenarios

Each scenario changes the generator and draws a new population with one shared new seed; the models and thresholds fitted on the base data score it unchanged. Because the legitimate segments are drawn before the bots and RTA users, `adaptive_bots` and `careful_rta` have the same legitimate players as `fresh_population`, and differ from it only in the changed segment.

| Scenario | Change |
|---|---|
| fresh_population | none: the same generator, a new seed |
| adaptive_bots | bots make deliberate mistakes (base error 0.10, more on hard spots), let time grow with difficulty with human-like spread, and keep human session lengths (2 to 6 h) and hours |
| careful_rta | RTA users consult the tool on a quarter of hard spots instead of half, with half the extra delay |
| legitimate_drift | regulars make half the mistakes (as if they studied with solvers) and play longer sessions (3 to 9 h); twice as many multi-tablers, taken from the recreational players |
